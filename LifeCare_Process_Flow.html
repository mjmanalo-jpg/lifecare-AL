Care Continuum — End-to-End Process Flow

From first assessment to a caregiver executing the daily routine. This document maps the five governed stages of the LifeCare Smart Living Management System (SLMS): how a resident is assessed, admitted, assigned a Level of Care, given an individualized Care Plan, and how that plan becomes a scheduled, shift-by-shift task list a caregiver actually completes at the bedside.

■ Prepared for: Facility Leadership & Clinical Staff ■ Version 1.0 · 04 Sep 2026 ■ Roles: Super Admin · Nurse · Care Manager · Caregiver · Admissions · Family

1

Pre-Admission Assessment

Screen the prospective resident and produce a provisional Level of Care (LOC) before admission.
Where does the resident come from?
Two ways to start an assessment
↳ Pre-fill from CRM

Type in the "Search admitted residents…" box to pull an existing CRM lead / admitted resident — name and profile fields auto-fill, no re-keying.

✎ New resident

Or just type a brand-new person directly into the form — no CRM record needed. The assessment can create the resident from scratch.

▼
Nurse / Care Manager / Super Admin
Open Resident Assessment → "New Assessment"
Launch the Resident Assessment v4.2 modal. Either path lands in the same form; work through the three layers: Layer 1 · Profile & History, Layer 2 · 14 Domains, Layer 3 · Evaluation.
Location: Resident Assessment (nurse / assessment hub)
▼
Nurse / Care Manager / Super Admin
Score the 14 domains, then Complete Assessment
Layer 2 scores mobility, ADLs, cognition, continence, behaviour, medical and other domains. A live "Running raw acuity" meter totals 0 / 56. Save Draft anytime, or Complete Assessment when all items across Layers 1–3 are done.
Engine: lifecare/assessment.ts (governed, unit-tested) — stored migration-free in assessments_v42 (origin = PREADMISSION)
▼
System
Score maps to a provisional Level of Care (Level 1–5)
The 0–56 total is banded into Level 1 (lowest support) through Level 5 (highest). This is advisory — it recommends, it does not lock the resident's level yet.
▼
Admissions decision
Is the facility able to safely serve this level of need?
✓ Suitable

Proceed to formal Admission & Onboarding (Stage 2), carrying the provisional level forward.

✕ Not suitable

Decline / refer out. Record the reason; the enquiry closes here.

Why it matters: the pre-admission score sets expectations for staffing, pricing and room fit before anyone signs. It is a screen, not the final ruling — the Final LOC is confirmed during onboarding.
2

Admission & Onboarding

Register the resident, run the full clinical assessment, and validate the Final Level of Care.
Admissions / Care Manager / Super Admin
Step 1 · Registration
Capture name, DOB (age auto-fills), date admitted, gender, contact, email and emergency contacts. Add a Family Sponsor — they get a scoped Family-portal login. Creates the resident record.
Location: Admission & Onboarding · "New Admission" wizard (4 steps, 0/4 complete)
▼
Nurse / Care Manager / Super Admin
Step 2 · Medical Assessment  →  Step 3 · Care Assessment
Record medical history, medications, surgeries and vaccines (Step 2). The Care Assessment step (Step 3) inherits the nurse/CM-validated Level of Care from the v4.2 assessment rather than silently recomputing.
Wizard steps: Registration → Medical Assess. → Care Assess. → Room & QR
▼
Nurse / Care Manager / Super Admin sign-off
Validate & lock the Final Level of Care
The clinician confirms (or overrides) the recommended level. Once validated, layer3.finalLevel becomes the sticky source of truth — every downstream board reads this, not the coarse enum.
▼
Admissions / Super Admin
Step 4 · Room & QR
Assign the room and generate the resident's QR wristband/record link. This completes the "New Admission" wizard.
▼
System
Resident is Active with a locked Final LOC
The validated level is recorded in the resident's LOC history and unlocks Care Plan creation. Onboarding is complete.
Key rule: validated beats stale. A nurse/CM-validated Final LOC always wins over an older screening score or the coarse care-level enum — this prevents a resident's level from reverting on refresh.
3

Care Plan Creation

Turn the approved Level of Care into an individualized plan with concrete interventions and tasks.
Trigger (New Review)
How is the Care Plan started?
Automatic

On LOC approval, a plan generates from the validated level — the resident leaves the "No plan yet" bucket.

Manual

Nurse/CM opens New Review on the Care Plan board, evaluates triggers, and makes the plan decision.

Location: Care Plan → Plan & Review · sub-tabs: Care Plans · New Review · Pending Approval · Reviews Due · History
▼
Generator
Build the base plan from the Level of Care
The generator maps the Final LOC to a standard set of goals, interventions and care tasks using the LifeCare rule data (the level → care-plan map and the care-task master list).
Engine: carePlanGen.ts / carePlanV42Gen.ts · data: assessment_to_care_plan.json, care_task_master.json
▼
Nurse / Care Manager / Super Admin
Individualize the plan (saved as a Draft)
Edit goals and interventions, add resident-specific tasks from the Task Library, and set assistance levels. Edits auto-save to the working Draft ("Awaiting review") as you type.
Editor: Care Plan Builder · draft store: care_plan_drafts · picklist: task_library.json
▼
Care Manager / Super Admin
Pending Approval → Release / Activate
The draft moves to Pending Approval; review-guards check it is complete for the level, then it is released and becomes ACTIVE. Each plan shows its Level, "Active since" date, and a "Next review" date, with View / Print.
Guards: carePlanReviewGuards.ts · release: carePlanRelease.ts
▼
System
ACTIVE plan — "Released & generating tasks"
The plan holds the interventions and the discrete care tasks. It generates the task set that feeds the 24-hour routine, and its review is queued under Reviews Due after the chosen interval.
Bridge to scheduling: a Care Plan is not just a document — it emits a concrete task set. Each task carries a target time-of-day window, which is what lets the routine and the caregiver schedule build themselves in the next stage.
4

Caregiver Schedule & Routine Assignment

Turn plan tasks into a 24-hour routine, then roster caregivers to residents per shift.
24-Hour Routine
Care Plan tasks fall into a 24-hour routine
On the Care Plan board's 24-Hour Routine tab, each active-plan task is placed into its time-of-day window (morning / afternoon / evening / night) — a repeating daily routine per resident.
Location: Care Plan → 24-Hour Routine · engine: lifecare/carePlanRoutine.ts · windows: routine_windows.json
▼
Nurse / Care Manager
Create a New Schedule (roster a caregiver to a shift)
On Staffing → Schedule, click New Schedule. Pick the date, the shift (e.g. AM Shift 6:00–14:00), and the caregiver. Dashboard shows Caregivers-on-today, Residents-covered, and Uncovered residents.
Location: Staffing → Schedule tab · lib: caregiverSchedule.ts
▼
Nurse / Care Manager
Assign the residents that caregiver will cover
In the same modal, tick the residents (e.g. "QA Test — Rm 100"), use Select all / Clear, add an optional note (e.g. "Cover for the AM medication pass"), and Save schedule.
"New schedule" modal · Residents (checkbox list) + Shift + Caregiver
▼
Scope lock
"Access their residents only on days they're scheduled"
Covering a resident on a shift is the assignment: that caregiver — and only that caregiver — receives those residents' routine tasks for that shift. No coverage = the resident shows as "uncovered".
▼
System
Each caregiver has a scoped, shift-specific task list
The routine is now live work: time-windowed, tied to covered residents, and visible only to the rostered carer for their shift.
Two things meet here: (1) the 24-Hour Routine says what and when for the resident; (2) the Schedule says who covers which residents on which shift. Assigning a caregiver to residents on a shift is what hands them that routine.
5

Today's Care — Execution & Documentation

The caregiver completes the assigned routine at the bedside and records what was done.
Caregiver
Open the Shift Dashboard
The carer sees only their assigned residents for the shift. Each card shows room, approved assistance level, code status / allergies, the current window (e.g. "15:00–17:00 · Afternoon"), and counts (Overdue / Due next). Filter by All · Due · Overdue · Up to date.
Location: Caregiver → Shift Dashboard (My Shift) · My Roster
▼
Caregiver
"Open routine" → the resident's care checklist
Opens "Today's care checklist for this resident" — the Caregiver Queue of routine tasks grouped by time window (e.g. Afternoon 15:00–17:00), each with the resident's Level shown. Bedside quick-logs sit alongside: Daily Log · ADL · Weight · MAR.
"Routine — [resident]" modal · per-task Complete + flag actions
▼
At each task
Was the task completed as planned?
✓ Complete

Tap Complete — the task counts toward the queue (e.g. 0/20 → done) and the resident moves toward "Up to date".

⚠ Flag / exception

Use the flag to record it couldn't be done as planned — it stays Overdue and carries into the shift endorsement.

▼
System
Completions → history, endorsement & monitoring
Completed and flagged tasks roll up into the resident's care history and the Endorsement Status at shift close, and feed monitoring trends — closing the loop toward the next reassessment.
Shift Close: Endorsement Status · escalations: Action Queue (Need Nurse / Help)
↺
Nurse / Care Manager
Reassess on schedule or on change
A periodic or triggered reassessment re-runs the v4.2 instrument (origin = ACUITY). A new Final LOC regenerates the Care Plan and routine — and the whole cycle repeats.
Back to Stage 1/3 via Resident Assessment & Care Plan → New Review
The loop closes: execution data is the evidence for the next assessment. Assess → admit → plan → schedule → execute → reassess is a continuous cycle, not a one-way line.
